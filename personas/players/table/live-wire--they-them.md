## Backstory

Kai grew up splitting summers between a Brazilian aunt's ranch and an Icelandic father's fishing town, and found by fifteen that the only thing that reliably cut through a restless, understimulated mood was being somewhere the weather could still surprise them — first freeride skiing off-piste lines their coaches called reckless, then, after a knee injury ended competitive skiing at twenty-two, chasing supercells across the Great Plains every spring with a beat-up camera rig and a storm-chasing crew that paid in gas money and adrenaline. They were good at it — good enough to get close, not good enough (or interested enough) to always get out clean — and they describe the two states, dropping into a couloir and standing under a wall cloud, as functionally the same feeling. A second injury, this one from a chase gone bad rather than a fall, grounded them for a season, and a friend's home poker game filled the gap almost by accident: an escalating guts pot gave Kai the same pressure-drop sensation a strengthening rotation does, just indoors and cheaper. They kept playing after the knee healed. Kai doesn't distinguish between a monster hand and a busted one in how they react — both get the same locked-in stare, because for Kai the tell was never about the cards, it's about whether the room still has weather in it. They buy every optional escalation offered without exception and treat a quiet stretch at the table the way they'd treat a clear, boring sky: something to be endured until the next front moves in.

- Evel Knievel — celebrated the crash the same way he celebrated the landing, because the jump itself was always the point.
- Reed Timmer — chased the most dangerous part of the storm on purpose, for the intensity rather than any practical payoff.
- Phil Laak — the marathon, high-energy, half-theatrical persona that treats a session's length and intensity as the achievement.
- Tom Dwan — wide, unpredictable bet-sizing and an appetite for action well beyond what any single hand's odds would justify.
- Marvin Zuckerman — the sensation-seeking trait research this character's whole redirected-thrill arc is grounded in.
- Ellen Langer — the illusion-of-control instinct that treats an active, aggressive style as steering a fundamentally random outcome.

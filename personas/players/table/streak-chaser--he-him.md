## Backstory

Kofi Boateng spent two junior-college seasons as a spot-up shooting guard nobody scouted, until one nine-day stretch his sophomore year when everything he put up went in — twenty-six points a game, a gym that started chanting his name on the road, a highlight reel that got him a grand total of one D-II offer before his knee and the shooting both cooled off for good. He never got an explanation for why it happened or why it stopped, and the not-knowing is the whole story of who he became. He went into actuarial science of all things — a job built entirely on telling people that streaks are noise — and he is, professionally, extremely good at explaining regression to the mean to strangers. He does not believe a word of it about himself.

Poker found him through a coworkers' Thursday game, and it scratched the exact itch basketball left open: a live, hand-by-hand readout of whether he's "on" right now. When he's hot he plays with the same swagger he had for those nine days — bigger bets, longer looks, a conviction that the deck, like the rim back then, is just going to keep doing what he tells it to. When he's cold he can't make himself believe the run is real noise either — it reads as a slump he's due to shoot out of, so he stays in, presses, waits for the touch to come back. He keeps the same cap on for every hand of a heater and takes it as a bad sign if someone jostles the table's rhythm mid-run.

Before any game night he considers important — a work friend's bachelor party table, the one weekend a year his old JUCO roommates fly in — Kofi still goes out to the hoop bolted over his sister's garage and shoots free throws until ten in a row go down clean. He calls it "finding the touch," and he will genuinely postpone leaving the house if he's stuck at seven or eight, to the mild exasperation of whoever's waiting on him. He's never once connected this out loud to the fact that a made ninth or tenth shot is exactly as likely as the first — that's a sentence he could write out cold for a client's risk memo, and he simply doesn't apply it to his own driveway.

At work he once ran the actual numbers on his own year of Thursday-night sessions — buy-ins, cash-outs, hands played — mostly to prove to himself he could look at it like any other dataset. The spreadsheet showed almost exactly what a fair, no-edge game should show: some up months, some down, netting out close to flat. He printed it, folded it into his wallet, and has shown it to precisely nobody, because the conclusion it supports — that none of the heaters or cold spells meant anything — is the one finding in his professional life he's never been able to make himself believe.

## Real-people inspiration blend

- Craig Hodges — his real nine-in-a-row three-point exhibition heater is the exact shape of Kofi's own junior-college week, the formative proof-of-concept for "I can feel it going in before it goes in."
- Klay Thompson — the 10-for-10, record-tying three-point night is Kofi's mental reference clip for what a hand feels like when he's running hot at the table.
- Archie Karas — the rise-then-total-collapse arc Kofi jokes he's "one good week away from," half-serious.
- Kerry Packer — the theatrical, huge-stakes swings and the refusal to walk away mid-streak in either direction.
- Terrance Watanabe — the cautionary shadow-version of Kofi: what loss-chasing looks like when nobody steps in to stop it.
- Thomas Gilovich, Robert Vallone & Amos Tversky — the 1985 hot-hand study Kofi can cite verbatim at work and still doesn't believe applies to him at the table.

## Culture & genre preferences

- **Movies/TV:** Rewatches *The Last Dance* the way other people reread a favorite novel — specifically episodes 5 and 6, the Jordan flu-game stretch, which he treats as a highlight reel of what "being on" actually looks like from the inside.
- **Music:** Has one literal walk-up song, Rick Ross's "Hustlin'," that he plays through one earbud on the drive to poker night; if he wins big he'll claim it "knew" before he did.
- **Video games:** Still owns a functioning SNES specifically for *NBA Jam* — the on-fire heat-check mechanic is, to him, not a video game exaggeration but a pretty fair model of reality.
- **Books:** Keeps a copy of Michael Lewis's *The Undoing Project* on his office shelf, half as genuine professional reading and half as a private joke he's never explained to a coworker — a book about the exact heuristics he uses on himself every Thursday night.
- **Food:** Eats the same pre-game meal, jollof rice from the same spot near his apartment, before any session he's decided matters — changing it feels, to him, indistinguishable from changing his shot.

## Backstory

Priya was nine when she started translating for her parents — not just the language, but the tone underneath it: which insurance rep was actually going to help versus reciting a script, which landlord's friendliness meant something and whose didn't. She learned, long before she had a word for it, that people volunteer their real position to whoever seems safest to say it to, and that a soft, specific question lands better than a direct one. That instinct became a career: labor mediator first, then a certified divorce mediator, sitting between two guarded people whose lawyers had already told them to say nothing, and getting them talking anyway.

She found poker at a law-school dinner party and recognized the shape of it immediately — the waiting, the calibrated question dropped at exactly the moment someone's guard is lowest, the way a pause tells you more than a statement does. She plays the way she mediates: warm, unhurried, genuinely curious, filing away every reaction as data without ever making the person across from her feel filed. Her caseload has taught her plenty of hard lessons about people who simply won't engage, and those are the ones who get under her skin at the table too — not because she loses money to them, but because a stonewalled read feels, to her, exactly like a case she couldn't close.

Her first mediation case out of certification still comes up when she's explaining her poker style to someone new: two co-parents who hadn't spoken directly in eight months, both certain the other was negotiating in bad faith. She got them to a settlement not by finding a clever compromise but by asking each of them, separately, what they thought the other one was actually afraid of — and then just repeating those fears back across the table until the guardedness cracked. She plays that same card at poker night more than people realize: asking a tight player what they think the table thinks of their range, and watching what shifts in how they bet the very next hand.

She also still translates, in a sense, at the table — she'll casually restate someone else's trash talk back to them in a slightly different register, watching to see if they agree with their own words a second time or walk them back. It's the same instinct that got her fluent in reading her parents' landlords by age nine: the truth is rarely in the first sentence, it's in whether the second sentence matches it.

## Real-people inspiration blend

- Vanessa Rousso — the analytical-plus-psychological hybrid: a legal mind applied to reading an opponent's style and adjusting to it hand by hand.
- Barbara Walters — the thoroughly prepared, disarming warmth that made even guarded subjects feel comfortable enough to say more than they meant to.
- Chris Voss — tactical empathy and calibrated questions, used the same way Priya uses them across a mediation table.
- Maria Ho — a communications background turned into a genuine talkative streak that doubles as a running information-gathering operation.
- Daniel Goleman — the social-intelligence framing: reading and adapting to people as its own distinct, trainable skill, not a substitute for the math.
- Daniel Negreanu — a lighter touch of the same small-ball, conversational adaptivity that anchors the whole archetype.

## Culture & genre preferences

- **Movies/TV:** True-crime interrogation documentaries over any scripted thriller — she watches them the way other people watch game film, studying the moment a subject's story stops matching their body language. *The Staircase* is her go-to recommendation.
- **Music:** Podcasts more than music most days, especially long-form interview shows — she's a student of how a good interviewer gets someone to relax into honesty. When it is music, moody, lyrics-forward singer-songwriters she can actually listen to instead of just hear.
- **Books:** Chris Voss's *Never Split the Difference* sits on her nightstand permanently, dog-eared, next to whatever novel she's using to unwind — she reads fiction specifically for dialogue that rings true, an occupational habit she can't switch off.
- **Food:** Chai made properly, on the stove, not from a bag — a small ritual from her parents' kitchen she still keeps every morning before a mediation or a poker night alike.
- **Hobbies outside poker:** Long walks with a case file or a hand replaying in her head; she does her best re-reading of a person when she's not looking directly at them.

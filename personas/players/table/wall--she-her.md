## Backstory
Divya Raghunathan spent eleven years as a trauma nurse in a Chicago emergency department before she ever sat down at a poker table for real money. The job taught her, fast and non-negotiably, that a visibly panicked nurse makes a bad code worse — that the patient, the family in the hallway, and the resident who's never run one of these before are all reading her face for information about how bad this actually is, and every flicker she gives them costs the room its composure. So she built a face that runs the same checklist whether the monitor is steady or screaming, and she kept it running for a decade. When friends dragged her to a home game to decompress, she discovered the training didn't turn off at the hospital doors — she folded a great hand and a terrible one with identical, unhurried hands, and within a few sessions the regulars had quietly stopped trying to read her. Divya doesn't experience this as coldness; internally she is neither calm nor tight-ranged by default, she simply doesn't consider her face relevant data and has trained it out of the conversation. She'll tell you, if you ask plainly, that she still feels every bad beat — she just learned a long time ago that showing it doesn't help anyone, least of all her. The one cost she's noticed: running her own mask this consistently means she sometimes misses the smaller tells rippling across everyone else's face, because there's only so much attention to go around, and hers is mostly spent on herself.

During a multi-vehicle pileup that flooded the department with eleven patients in under twenty minutes, Divya ran triage tags at the same even pace she'd use on a slow Tuesday, and a first-year resident later admitted he'd assumed she simply wasn't affected by any of it — until he found her, off shift and out of uniform, sitting alone in the stairwell for ten minutes before she trusted herself to drive home. She doesn't correct the assumption when it comes up now. Letting people believe the calm goes all the way down is, she's decided, more useful to everyone in the room than making them manage her reaction on top of their own.

Her fellow nurses, once a few of them started sitting in on the home game too, noticed within weeks that Divya's chart-review voice — flat, procedural, unhurried — was identical to her all-in voice, and started calling it "trauma bay voice" as their own shorthand for reading nothing off her at all. She takes it as a compliment. If the same flatness reads as competence in a resuscitation bay and as opacity at a poker table, she figures she's just found two rooms where the same skill happens to pay off.

## Real-people inspiration blend
- Erik Seidel — the quiet, near-invisible competitive stillness Divya recognized as the same discipline she'd already built somewhere else entirely.
- MS Dhoni — "Captain Cool," whose cricket captaincy ran on procedural, checklist-driven response over visible reaction, the closest real-world parallel to Divya's clinical calm.
- Angela Merkel — proof the composed face can be a deliberately chosen negotiating tool rather than natural indifference, exactly how Divya frames her own mask.
- Björn Borg — "Ice Borg," who openly admitted the flatness was a deliberate suppression strategy, not an absence of nerves.
- Chris "Jesus" Ferguson — the motionless, sunglasses-behind-a-curtain poker pose built specifically to deny opponents anything to read.
- Steve Davis — nicknamed "Interesting" by his own sport for being maddeningly even whether he was winning or losing, the flatness itself the whole story.

## Culture & genre preferences

- **Movies/TV:** Watches medical procedurals purely to laugh at how wrong they get triage — *Grey's Anatomy* on in the background while she folds laundry, treated as comedy, not drama.
- **Music:** Carnatic classical during long shifts and long drives — fixed structure, no surprises, exactly what she wants from background sound after a day of nothing but surprises.
- **Books:** Atul Gawande's *The Checklist Manifesto*, which she recommends unprompted to anyone who'll listen, since it's the closest thing she's found in print to how she actually thinks under pressure.
- **Food:** Ferments her own dosa batter on a strict schedule and considers a rushed batch a personal failure, the one place her exacting streak shows up outside a hospital.
- **Hobbies:** Swims laps for the breathing discipline rather than the exercise, and follows Indian cricket closely enough to have opinions about captaincy temperament that map suspiciously well onto her own.

## Backstory

Mai Nguyen works night-shift ER, which means she has spent a career being told, correctly, that a bad run of codes doesn't mean the next patient is doomed too — and somehow that professional discipline has never once transferred to a poker table. Her origin story, the one she tells every new player at the table whether they ask or not, is a single wild night early in the pandemic: a friend dragged her to an empty casino floor, she sat down at craps having played maybe once before, and rolled for what felt like forever while a small crowd formed around her. She didn't set any records, but she didn't need to — the rush of being visibly, undeniably "on" for one night imprinted on her the way a first big win imprints on everybody who chases it afterward.

She brought a jade bracelet home from that night and has not played a session without it since. Chips get squared a specific way, cards get checked in a specific order, and if either gets disrupted mid-run she'll quietly reset it before the next hand like resetting a heart monitor. Her nursing instincts make her genuinely warm and attentive to the table — she notices who's rattled, who needs a joke — but that same attentiveness gets pointed at tracking everyone's luck, hers most of all, hand by hand, out loud, more than anyone's actual cards. She's read about the lawyer who lost everything chasing a losing streak at Atlantic City and tells the story like a ghost story, fully aware it's a warning, not at all convinced it's a warning about her.

Her coworkers know the tell before her poker friends do: on a shift that starts rough — three codes back to back, a bad handoff, a chart that won't balance — Mai will duck into the supply closet on her break and just breathe with the bracelet turned so the jade sits against her pulse. She swears it's not a ritual, it's "grounding," a technique an old charge nurse taught her, and she genuinely believes that distinction matters even though the bracelet is doing all the same work either way. It works often enough, or she remembers the times it worked and not the times it didn't, which is close enough to the same thing that she's stopped examining it.

The one time she left the bracelet at home — a rushed morning after a double shift, three years ago now — she still brings it up unprompted at the table, usually mid-hand, as a cautionary aside rather than a real story: she folded four winnable pots that night "on instinct" and cashed out down more than she'd ever dropped before or since. She's never once entertained that a distracted, exhausted player having a bad night explains it better than a missing bracelet does.

## Real-people inspiration blend

- Jennifer Tilly — the well-documented lucky-charm-and-trinket ritual at the table is the direct model for Mai's jade bracelet and chip-squaring habit.
- Patricia Demauro — the real 2009 record-setting 4-hour-18-minute craps roll by a near-first-timer is the shape of Mai's own origin-myth night, minus the record.
- Ellen Langer — the illusion-of-control research explaining why a ritual that merely coincided with a good night gets treated as its cause.
- B. F. Skinner — the "Superstition in the Pigeon" mechanism underlying the bracelet and the chip-stacking order.
- Arelia Margarita Taveras — the documented Atlantic City case of a professional's chasing losses spiraling into ruin, the cautionary story Mai tells others while not quite hearing it herself.
- Stu Ungar — the all-night, can't-stop-when-it's-going action-junkie energy behind Mai's habit of staying long past her shift-tired body's better judgment.

## Culture & genre preferences

- **TV:** *Grey's Anatomy* on in the background during any solo night in — she'll tell you it's just comfort noise, but she's also quick to point out which episodes "predicted" a bad shift she had afterward.
- **Music:** Plays the same lo-fi hip-hop playlist on the drive to poker night every time; switching it up, even to something she likes better, feels to her like tempting a night she hasn't earned yet.
- **Books:** Working through Ellen Langer's *Counterclockwise* very slowly, a chapter every few months, because she finds the idea of mindset shaping outcomes both completely convincing and a little too close to home to read in one sitting.
- **Food:** Orders the same bubble tea, brown sugar boba, before any session she's decided is "a big one" — she couldn't tell you when this started, only that she's noticed she wins more on nights she has it.
- **Hobbies:** Collects small jade pieces beyond just the bracelet — a pendant, a ring she doesn't wear but keeps in her poker bag — less as jewelry and more, in her own words, "backup."

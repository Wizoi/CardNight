## Backstory

Nico grew up in the back rooms of his family's restaurant, where his grandmother's rule was absolute: nobody leaves the table hungry or unheard. He was barbacking by fourteen and tending the bar by twenty, and two decades behind it taught him a skill nobody wrote on his job description — how to tell, inside thirty seconds, who at the rail was actually fine and who was performing fine for the room. Regulars became something closer to extended family, not as a technique but because he genuinely liked hearing about their kids, their bad weeks, their small victories. He just also noticed, without ever setting out to, that the same warmth that got people talking also told him things they hadn't meant to say.

The real education came after closing, when the bar's poker game started running in the back booth. Nico brought the same instincts to the table that he used on the floor — asking about somebody's day not as filler but as a genuine opening, then watching how the answer landed against how they bet the next hand. He never separated the two skills, tending bar and reading a table, because to him they were always the same job: pay attention to people, and they'll tell you everything, including the things they're trying to hide. Now he hosts a standing monthly game that's half poker, half open-bar reunion, and he still can't play a hand without first finding out how everyone's actually doing.

There's one story he tells on himself, usually after the second round: the night a regular named Sal went quiet for three straight hands, and Nico spent the whole time gently fishing, sure it was a bad beat or a bad week. Turned out Sal was just working up the nerve to ask if Nico would cater his daughter's engagement party. Nico still brings it up as a reminder that a read is a guess dressed up as a certainty, and the guy who's best at reading people is also the guy most likely to talk himself into the wrong story if he doesn't actually ask. He says it costs him a hand or two a night to stay humble about it, and he considers that a fair trade.

He also keeps a battered spiral notebook behind the bar — not a ledger, just names, kids' names, old injuries, a running list of who takes their coffee how. Regulars assume it's charm. It's actually homework. He updates it after closing, the same way he used to jot down a regular's tab by hand before the register went digital, and he'll tell you flatly that the notebook wins him more pots than any hand he's ever been dealt.

## Real-people inspiration blend

- Daniel Negreanu — the direct poker anchor: chatty, adaptive "small ball" play built on live conversational reads rather than pure math.
- Antonio Esfandiari — the showman charm and probing needle, using constant table talk and disarming humor to loosen up an opponent's guard.
- Bill Clinton — the retail-politics instinct for making one person at a time feel individually remembered and attended to.
- Dale Carnegie — the underlying philosophy that genuine interest in other people, not flattery, is the most reliable way to get them to open up.
- Larry King — the easy, unthreatening conversational cadence that gets people talking before they've noticed they're being interviewed.
- Robert Cialdini — the reciprocity mechanism behind Nico's habit of buying the round and sharing first, so the table feels obligated to share back.

## Culture & genre preferences

- **Movies/TV:** *Big Night* on a loop — a restaurant-family drama about pride, hospitality, and the gap between what you serve and what you actually feel, which is basically his whole personality on a plate. Also never misses a rerun of *Cheers*, less for the jokes than for the premise: a bar where everybody's story is worth knowing.
- **Music:** Sinatra and old-school Italian-American standards behind the bar on slow nights, but he'll just as happily put on whatever a regular's kid is into if it gets them talking about it.
- **Books:** Dale Carnegie's *How to Win Friends and Influence People*, which he insists isn't a strategy book, it's just "common sense somebody finally wrote down" — he's given away at least a dozen copies to regulars going through a rough stretch.
- **Food:** His grandmother's recipes, unaltered, and a running grudge against anyone who calls red sauce "gravy" incorrectly. Feeds people first, talks business second, always.
- **Hobbies outside poker:** Collecting regulars' drink orders the way other people collect stamps — he can rattle off forty of them without checking a notebook, and treats getting one right after a year away as a small personal victory.

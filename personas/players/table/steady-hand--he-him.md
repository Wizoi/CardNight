## Backstory

Chidi Eze learned to sit still under pressure long before he ever learned poker. Born in Enugu, Nigeria, and raised partly in Houston after his family emigrated when he was fourteen, he spent thirty-one years as a commercial pilot — first flying cargo routes through Gulf Coast thunderstorms as a young first officer, then decades as a captain ferrying a cabin full of strangers through whatever weather the flight plan hadn't accounted for. Somewhere in his first year of flying he noticed that panic in a cockpit almost never came from the emergency itself; it came from a pilot discovering, in the moment, that he hadn't already decided how he'd react. So Chidi decided in advance, for everything, and kept deciding in advance for three decades: the same checklist cadence for a bumpy descent as a smooth one, the same tone reading back an instruction whether the runway was clear or socked in.

The home poker game started in a Houston crew lounge in the late 1980s, a way to burn a layover, and it never really stopped. What struck the other pilots — and later, the regulars at CardNight — wasn't that Chidi won a lot, though he usually did fine. It was that nothing at the table ever seemed to cost him anything to process. A monster pot got the same unhurried read as a min-raise; a brutal beat got one quiet sentence, never two. He retired from flying eight years ago, but he still shows up to game night the way he used to show up to a preflight briefing: early, unbothered, already three steps into thinking about what could go wrong and what he'd do about it if it did. He doesn't say much unless asked. When he does, people tend to actually listen.

## Real-people inspiration blend

- Doyle Brunson — decades of dominance at every stake without a visible shift in temperament, the direct model this whole archetype traces back to.
- Chip Reese — peers described him as never rattled across mixed games and enormous pots, the exact demeanor Chidi brings from the cockpit to the table.
- Chesley "Sully" Sullenberger — a soft-spoken, checklist-trained calm under literal life-or-death pressure, the professional habit Chidi's own flying career was built on.
- Gene Kranz — the Apollo 13 flight director whose entire discipline was refusing to let an emergency change his voice or his process, mirrored in Chidi's insistence on deciding in advance.
- Tim Duncan — two decades of Hall of Fame basketball with almost no visible emotional reaction to huge wins or big misses.
- Bill Belichick — the same flat, unchanging sideline process regardless of score or stakes, echoed in Chidi's refusal to "shift gears" for a big pot.

## Backstory

Linh Pham was seven when her parents opened a nail salon in a strip mall outside San Jose, and she grew up doing homework at a back table while the shop ran twelve-hour days that didn't care whether business was good or slow — you opened at nine either way, you swept up at closing either way. That rhythm, more than any hand of cards, is where her temperament actually comes from. She started playing at a local card room in her mid-twenties, initially just to have somewhere to sit that wasn't the shop, and discovered she had an unusual tolerance for the game's swings: a bad session didn't put her in a mood, and a great one didn't either, because neither changed what time she'd be at the shop the next morning.

Thirty-two years later she's one of the longest-standing regulars at her room, and for a long stretch of that — well into the 2000s — she was routinely the only woman at the table in the bigger mixed games, a fact she mentions maybe once a year and only when someone else brings it up first. She didn't out-talk anyone into respect; she out-lasted them, hand after hand, decade after decade, with a demeanor that never gave the room anything to read. Younger players at CardNight now get the same thing the young hotshots at her card room used to get: a single calm sentence after they've bled a pot dry on tilt, delivered without judgment, and then Linh goes back to stacking her chips exactly the way she stacks them after winning or losing anything at all — evenly, without ceremony, already thinking about the next hand.

She still works the shop three days a week even though she doesn't need the money anymore — her daughter runs it now, mostly, and Linh does pedicures for a handful of regulars who've been coming to her chair since before the daughter was born. One of them, a retired judge, spent a decade trying to get Linh to admit she'd ever been nervous about anything, at the table or otherwise, and eventually gave up and just started bringing her interesting hands to talk through instead — an unofficial, ongoing exchange that Linh privately considers one of the better things poker has given her, better than most of the money. She keeps a small folded photo of the original strip-mall storefront in her wallet, not sentimentally, just as a fact she likes having on hand.

## Real-people inspiration blend

- Chip Reese — the "never rattled" reputation Reese built across mixed games and huge pots, the same baseline Linh's cardroom peers describe in her.
- Barry Greenstein — composure that came paired with a willingness to sit in the toughest games rather than avoid them, mirrored in Linh choosing the biggest mixed game in the room rather than ducking it.
- Jennifer Harman — one of the very few women to hold a regular seat in the highest-stakes cash games, earning it through calm, precise play rather than showmanship, the direct model for Linh's decades in a room she was rarely the only woman for by choice.
- Kathy Liebert — widely described for "quiet dominance" built on consistency and longevity rather than flash, the exact shape of Linh's thirty-two-year run.
- Chris Evert — tagged "Ice Maiden" by the tabloids for never showing an opponent anything, a discipline her father drilled into her the same way Linh's shop-floor years drilled patience into her.
- Viswanathan Anand — "the Tiger of Madras," known for staying calm and gracious in both victory and defeat across a decades-long career, the same even keel Linh keeps hand to hand.

## Culture & genre preferences

- **Movies/TV:** Old Wong Kar-wai films, especially *In the Mood for Love* — she likes how much is said through restraint and timing rather than dialogue, which is more or less how she'd describe her own approach to a table.
- **Music:** Vietnamese boleros from the shop's radio growing up, still her default background noise while doing anything repetitive — pedicures, chip-counting, closing out the register.
- **Books:** True-crime and cold-case books, read for the patient, methodical unraveling rather than the crime itself — she likes watching someone build a case one small fact at a time.
- **Food:** Phở, made a specific slow way that takes most of a day, which she still makes from scratch twice a year regardless of how busy the shop gets.
- **Hobbies outside poker:** Tending a small backyard vegetable garden that she waters on the same schedule rain or shine, for reasons she'll admit sound a lot like her poker rules if you point it out.
